Why twists?

Where one functional equation stops, and what the Ramanujan-sum twists add
q = 1, 2: one translate, the relation moves a point up q ≥ 3: the relation only controls a sum over a/q not covered, or two semicircles only touch fundamental domain (q = 1 only)
  • One functional equation relates \(f(z)\) and \(f(-1/(Nz))\): a half-turn about \(i/\sqrt N\).
  • Inside the semicircles \(|z-n|<1/\sqrt N\) it moves points up (a reflection in the semicircle, then in a vertical line).
  • For \(N\le4\) the semicircles cover the line; the shaded fundamental domain has area \(2\arcsin(\sqrt N/2)\).
  • At \(N=5\) a gap opens at \(\tfrac12\).
  • The fundamental domain reaches the line: its area is infinite.
  • Over the gap there is no control: for \(N\ge5\) the solutions form an infinite-dimensional space.
  • The Euler product gives a functional equation for every Ramanujan-sum twist \(c_q\), of conductor \(\operatorname{lcm}(N,q^2)\).
  • For \(q=2\) only \(z+\tfrac12\) occurs: one more half-turn, about \(\tfrac12+i/\sqrt{\operatorname{lcm}(N,4)}\).
  • It closes the gap.
  • With \(q\le2\) the line stays covered up to \(N=8\).
  • At \(N=9\) the semicircles only touch, at \(\tfrac13\) and \(\tfrac23\).
  • These are the two cusps of \(\Gamma_0(9)\) with denominator \(3\).
  • At \(N=11\) gaps open that no single half-turn reaches.
  • \(N=10\) and \(N=12\) are still covered with \(q\le2\); from \(N=13\) on, never.
  • Twists with \(q\ge3\) reach the gaps.
  • But each controls only the sum of \(f\) over \(z+a/q\), \(\gcd(a,q)=1\): the hollow dots turn together.
  • Spanning theorem: the sums can be untangled exactly when \(16\nmid N\) and \(p^2\nmid N\) for odd \(p\).
  • At \(N=9\) they cannot be untangled.
  • Only one semicircle contains \(\tfrac13\): the \(q=3\) one centred there; all others are at best tangent.
  • It sees only \(f(z+\tfrac13)+f(z+\tfrac23)\), where \(+1\) near \(\tfrac13\) and \(-1\) near \(\tfrac23\) cancel.
  • Choose \(N\) and the largest \(q\) with the sliders.
  • Hover (or touch) to see a point reflected in its semicircle, then in a vertical line.

One functional equation

  • For a sequence \((f_n)\) put \(f(z)=\sum_{n\ge1} f_n n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi i n z}\). The functional equation of \(L_f(s)\) says $$f\!\left(-\frac{1}{Nz}\right)=\epsilon_f\,(-i\sqrt N z)^k f(z).$$
  • \(z\mapsto-1/(Nz)\) is a half-turn of the hyperbolic plane about \(i/\sqrt N\).
  • \(\operatorname{Im}(-1/(Nz))=\operatorname{Im}(z)/(N|z|^2)\), so it raises \(z\) exactly when \(|z|<1/\sqrt N\).
  • With \(f(z+1)=f(z)\): values near the line are determined by values higher up wherever the semicircles \(|z-n|<1/\sqrt N\) reach, which is everywhere iff \(N\le4\).
  • For \(N\le4\) the region \(0\le\operatorname{Re}z\le1\), \(|z|\ge1/\sqrt N\), \(|z-1|\ge1/\sqrt N\) is a fundamental domain of area \(2\arcsin(\sqrt N/2)\): \(\pi/3,\pi/2,2\pi/3,\pi\).

Twists by Ramanujan sums

  • \(c_q(n)=\sum_{\gcd(a,q)=1}e^{2\pi i an/q}\).
  • If \(L_f\) has an Euler product, its \(c_q\)-twist has a functional equation of conductor \(\ell_N(q)=\operatorname{lcm}(N,q^2)\).
  • In terms of \(f\), it relates \(\sum_{\gcd(a,q)=1}f(z+\tfrac aq)\) to the same sum at \(-1/(\ell_N(q)z)\): one half-turn applied to all the translates at once.
  • It raises points inside the semicircles of radius \(1/\sqrt{\ell_N(q)}\) centred at the fractions \(a/q\).

The spanning theorem (no Euler factors)

  • For \(q\mid Q\) and \(u\in\mathbb Z\): \(R^Q_{q,u}=\sum_{\gcd(a,q)=1}e_{u+aQ/q}\), legal if \(\max\{|u|,|u+1|\}<Q/\sqrt{\ell_N(q)}\).
  • A legal row is one relation used on the cell \([\tfrac uQ,\tfrac{u+1}{Q}]\), shifted to each \(a/q\): the cell must lie under the semicircle.
  • Some \(Q\) makes the legal rows span \(\mathbb Q^{\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z}\) iff \(16\nmid N\) and \(p^2\nmid N\) for every odd prime \(p\).
  • Spanning means every value of \(f\) near the line is a combination of values strictly higher up.

At an excluded level the points \(a/s\) (\(s=p\) with \(p^2\mid N\), or \(s=4\) with \(16\mid N\)) lie inside exactly one semicircle each, the one with \(q=s\) centred there. This was checked in exact arithmetic for all \(q\le200\) at \(N=9,16,18,25,27,36\).

  • From A. R. Booker, M. Lee and N. Zubrilina, Towards a twistless converse theorem.
  • Semicircles are drawn for all \(q\) up to the slider value and all \(a\) with \(\gcd(a,q)=1\).
  • Between integer values of \(N\) the radii are interpolated; the label shows the nearest integer.
  • The moving points follow the half-turns exactly: a reflection in the semicircle, then in a vertical line (through the centre or half an integer away, the same map modulo 1).
