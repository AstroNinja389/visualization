From semicircles to rows

How the twisted relations become the spanning theorem
q = 1, 2: a row with a single cell q ≥ 3: a row with one cell for each a/q cells no single relation reaches
Fix \(Q=44\) and cut the circle \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) into \(44\) cells. A point \(z\) just above the line has one translate \(z+\frac n{44}\) in every cell (the dots), and the values \(f(z+\frac n{44})\) form a vector with one entry per cell. Level \(N=11\).
The functional equation itself (\(q=1\)) moves a translate up when its whole cell lies under the semicircle at \(0\), of radius \(1/\sqrt{11}\). Each such cell \(u\) gives a legal row with a single \(1\): the value in that cell is controlled from above.
The twist by \(c_2\) does the same under the semicircle at \(\tfrac12\), of radius \(1/\sqrt{44}\): twelve more single-cell rows.
Six cells are left: \(13,14,15\) and their mirror images \(28,29,30\). No single relation reaches them. This is the gap of Hecke's picture, cut into cells.
For \(q\ge3\) the \(c_q\)-twist only controls the sum over all the translates at the fractions \(a/q\): its row \(R_{q,u}\) has a \(1\) in each of the cells \(u+44a/q\). It is legal when the cell \(u\) lies under the semicircle at \(0\) of radius \(1/\sqrt{\operatorname{lcm}(11,q^2)}\); then all those cells lie under the semicircles at the \(a/q\), and all of them move up together.
Untangling a gap cell: \(R_{4,2}\) has the cells \(13\) and \(35\), and \(R_{1,-9}\) is the cell \(35\) alone, so \(R_{4,2}-R_{1,-9}=e_{13}\). The value in cell \(13\) is the \(q=4\) sum minus a value that \(q=1\) controls. Cell \(14\) needs eleven rows, including the ten-cell row \(R_{11,-2}\).
The \(52\) legal rows have rank \(44\): every cell is a combination of legal rows, so every value near the real line is controlled from above. This is the spanning theorem at \(N=11\), \(Q=44\), and it is what the reconstruction lemma uses.
At \(N=9\), with \(Q=36\), the four cells touching \(\tfrac13\) and \(\tfrac23\) are each met by exactly one legal row: \(R_{3,-1}=e_{11}+e_{23}\) and \(R_{3,0}=e_{12}+e_{24}\). So \(+1\) beside \(\tfrac13\) and \(-1\) beside \(\tfrac23\) is invisible to every row; the rank is \(34\), and the kernel is spanned by \(e_{12}-e_{24}\) and \(e_{23}-e_{11}\).

The dictionary. Fix \(N\) and \(Q\), and for a point \(z=x+iy\) with \(x\) in the cell \([\tfrac jQ,\tfrac{j+1}Q]\) let \(v(n)=f\bigl(z+\frac{n-j}{Q}\bigr)\), one entry for each cell \(n\bmod Q\). The functional equation of the \(c_q\)-twist relates \(\sum_{\gcd(a,q)=1}f(\beta+\frac aq)\) to the same sum at \(-1/(\ell_N(q)\beta)\), with \(\ell_N(q)=\operatorname{lcm}(N,q^2)\). Taking \(\beta=z+\frac{u-j}{Q}\), the left side is $$\sum_{\gcd(a,q)=1}v\Bigl(u+\frac{aQ}{q}\Bigr)=\langle R^Q_{q,u},v\rangle,\qquad R^Q_{q,u}=\sum_{\gcd(a,q)=1}e_{u+aQ/q},$$ and the right side is higher up exactly when \(\beta\) is under the semicircle \(|\beta|<1/\sqrt{\ell_N(q)}\). Requiring this for every point of the cell \(u\) is the legality condition \(\max\{|u|,|u+1|\}

Spanning. If \(e_j=\sum A_j(q,u)R^Q_{q,u}\) over legal rows, then \(f(z)=v(j)=\sum A_j(q,u)\langle R^Q_{q,u},v\rangle\) is a combination of sums that the twisted functional equations express through values of \(f\) higher up. The spanning theorem says such a \(Q\) exists exactly when \(16\nmid N\) and \(p^2\nmid N\) for every odd prime \(p\).

Ranks are exact over \(\mathbb Q\) for every prefix of the rows (52 rows at \(N=11\), 80 at \(N=9\)). The combinations shown at \(N=11\) are the certificate of the cascade page: each minimises \(\sum|A_j(q,u)|\varphi(q)\), all coefficients are \(\pm1\), and each identity was checked in exact arithmetic. At \(N=9\), \(Q=36\), the statement about the four cells was checked on all 80 legal rows.

From A. R. Booker, M. Lee and N. Zubrilina, Towards a twistless converse theorem. The dots are the translates of one point \(z\) at height \(0.025\), one per cell; the short arrows mark the translates a relation moves up.
