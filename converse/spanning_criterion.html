The spanning criterion

Which Euler factors the argument needs

Fix \(N\). For \(Q\in\mathbb N\) the legal rows are the vectors \(R^Q_{q,u}=\sum_{\gcd(a,q)=1}e_{u+aQ/q}\) with \(q\mid Q\) and \(\max\{|u|,|u+1|\}<Q/\sqrt{\operatorname{lcm}(N,q^2)}\), one for each twisted functional equation that moves the cell \(u\) up. A prime \(p\) also contributes the complete rows \(C^Q_{p,u}=\sum_{a\bmod p}e_{u+aQ/p}\), which come from the Euler factor at \(p\). A finite set of primes \(P\) is vanishing for \(N\) if, for some \(Q\) divisible by every \(p\in P\), the legal rows together with the complete rows \(C^Q_{p,u}\), \(p\in P\), span \(\mathbb Q^{\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z}\).

Spanning theorem. \(P\) is vanishing for \(N\) if and only if every \(s>2\) that is divisible by all the primes of \(P\) satisfies $$s^2>N_s,\qquad N_s=\gcd(N,s^\infty)=\prod_{p\mid s}p^{\operatorname{ord}_p N}.$$

Three special cases

  1. \(P=\varnothing\) is vanishing if and only if \(N\) is divisible neither by \(16\) nor by \(p^2\) for an odd prime \(p\): the legal rows alone span.
  2. The set of primes dividing \(N\) is vanishing if and only if \(N\mid 8\) or \(\operatorname{rad}(N)>\sqrt N\). For these primes the Euler factor pins \(f_p\) down to finitely many values, so this is the case in which the paper needs no growth condition on the coefficients.
  3. For every prime \(p>\sqrt N\), the set \(\{p\}\) is vanishing: no \(s\le\sqrt N\) is divisible by \(p\), and every larger \(s\) has \(s^2>N\ge N_s\). Here \(f_p\) is an unknown Hecke eigenvalue; this case gives the finiteness theorem at every level, where the growth exponent keeps \(f_p\) in a bounded set.

Why \(s\) blocks: if \(s>2\), every prime of \(P\) divides \(s\), and \(s^2\le N_s\), then for every \(Q\) divisible by \(s\) the vector with the value \(\sin(2\pi m/s)\) at the position \(mQ/s\) (and \(0\) elsewhere) is orthogonal to every legal row and to every complete row \(C^Q_{p,u}\) with \(p\in P\). This is the imaginary part of the vector in the paper's proof of necessity, built from an \(s\)-th root of unity. It is drawn below the chart when \(P\) is blocked.

From A. R. Booker, M. Lee and N. Zubrilina, Towards a twistless converse theorem. Only \(s\le\sqrt N\) can block, since \(N_s\le N\); the chart shows \(s\) from \(3\) to \(\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\). The criterion as computed here was checked for every \(N\le2000\) against the three special cases, and for \(N\le 95\) against the paper's table of the smallest \(Q\) for \(P=\varnothing\), which is quoted in the verdict.
